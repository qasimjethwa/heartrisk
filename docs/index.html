Limitations — read before trusting any number here

  • Old, narrow cohort. The Framingham-style data come from a mid-20th-century US town cohort (ages 32–70). It does not represent today's global population, and performance on other populations is untested here.
  • Imbalanced outcome. Only about 15% of training rows developed CHD within 10 years. Accuracy alone is misleading; ROC-AUC, F1 and the confusion matrix matter more.
  • Missing data. Glucose was missing in 388 of 4,240 rows; other fields had smaller gaps. Medians were imputed from the training split only, never from the test split.
  • No causal claim. A scenario that lowers an input number changes the model's output. It does not prove your personal risk would fall by that amount, or that any intervention is safe or appropriate for you.
  • Binary sex coding, no former-smoker category, no family history beyond prevalent stroke/hypertension — all inherited from the source dataset.
  • Not validated clinically. No clinical validation, regulatory review or prospective testing was done for this project.

FAQs

Is this a diagnosis?

No. It is an educational demonstration of a machine-learning model. Only a clinician, with your full history and examinations, can assess your health.

Is my health data stored or sent anywhere?

No. Inputs live only in this page's memory while it is open. There is no account, no browser storage, no analytics and no external request — the page loads only its own stylesheet, script and model file from this site.

Why do two models appear in the metrics?

Logistic regression and random forest were both trained and compared honestly. The model shipped in the browser is the one that balanced calibrated performance, faithful browser export and explainability — see Methodology for the choice and its reason.

Can I trust a scenario that says risk falls by, say, 6 points?

Only as a model output. It is the model's estimate if that one input were different and everything else stayed the same. Real life does not hold everything else constant, and the model may be wrong for you.
